# German Historical and Conceptual Terms

This file records German terms whose historical, intellectual, institutional, legal, political, or literary meaning materially affects the Persian translation of *Revisiting Zero Hour 1945*.

It is not a general German–Persian dictionary.

Its purpose is to preserve the conceptual identity of historically significant German terminology and to document why a particular Persian rendering is chosen.

The translation workflow for these terms is:

```text
German concept
      ↓
historical / disciplinary meaning
      ↓
author's English representation
      ↓
function in the current passage
      ↓
Persian candidates
      ↓
Adel's decision
```

Do not infer historical meaning from the German word alone. Substantive conceptual explanations must be supported by sources actually checked.

---

## 1. When to create an entry

Create an entry here when:

- the source explicitly uses a German word or phrase;
- an English expression represents a historically specific German concept;
- the term belongs to German political, intellectual, academic, legal, or literary history;
- a simple dictionary equivalent would conceal an important distinction;
- the author contrasts two related German terms;
- the same term recurs and its translation needs consistency;
- uncertainty about the German concept materially affects the Persian translation.

Ordinary recurring vocabulary belongs primarily in:

`translation-references/GLOSSARY.md`

A term may appear in both files when necessary.

---

## 2. Status system

Every entry with a proposed or previously used Persian equivalent must have one of the following translation-decision statuses. Research state is recorded separately; it does not measure approval or establish a historical interpretation.

### candidate

The interpretation or Persian equivalent is plausible but has not yet been explicitly approved by Adel.

### approved

Adel has explicitly approved the Persian equivalent for the stated sense and context.

### reconsider

A previous choice must be reviewed because new context, evidence, or a different sense has appeared.

### retired

An older interpretation or equivalent is retained for traceability but should no longer be used.

Approval applies to a specific sense and context. It does not automatically govern every occurrence of the same German word.

---

## 3. Evidence discipline

Keep the following categories separate:

- **literal meaning**
- **dictionary evidence**
- **historical/conceptual evidence**
- **author-specific usage**
- **mentor interpretation**
- **Persian usage evidence**
- **Adel's translation decision**

Never claim that a source supports an interpretation unless the relevant entry or passage was actually checked.

If the historical or conceptual meaning is not sufficiently verified, mark:

**German concept source check needed**

Do not replace uncertainty with an invented explanation.

---

## 4. Translation principles

### Preserve meaningful distinctions

Do not collapse related German terms merely because their Persian equivalents appear similar.

For example:

```text
Stunde Null ≠ automatically Nullpunkt
```

The distinction must be investigated in context before deciding whether Persian should preserve it.

### Do not automatically leave German untranslated

The purpose of this file is not to fill the Persian translation with German terminology.

Possible strategies include:

1. Persian equivalent only;
2. Persian equivalent + German form at first significant occurrence;
3. German term + short Persian explanation;
4. temporary retention of the German term when no adequate Persian equivalent has yet been established.

### First significant occurrence

When conceptually useful, use:

```text
Persian equivalent + German term
```

Example format:

> «ساعت صفر» (*Stunde Null*)

Later occurrences may use the established Persian equivalent alone unless the German form becomes analytically important again.

### Historical explanation belongs primarily in analysis

Do not overload the Persian translation with explanatory material merely because a concept has a complicated history.

Historical explanation normally belongs in:

- mentor analysis;
- `GERMAN-CONCEPTS.md`;
- translator's notes or endnotes when editorially justified.

## 7. Relationship to the project glossary

Use:

```text

→ practical recurring translation choices
```

Use:

```text
GERMAN-CONCEPTS.md
→ historical and conceptual investigation behind selected choices
```

Example:

```text
GERMAN-CONCEPTS.md
Wiedergutmachung
→ investigate historical/legal/political meaning

GLOSSARY.md
Wiedergutmachung
→ record the Persian rendering chosen for the relevant sense
```

Avoid duplicating a long conceptual explanation in both files. Cross-reference instead.

---

## 8. Working rule for the mentor

Whenever a relevant German concept appears:

1. identify the exact German form;
2. locate its role in the sentence and argument;
3. determine whether the English wording translates or interprets it;
4. check historical or specialist evidence when necessary;
5. identify Persian candidates;
6. explain what each candidate preserves or loses;
7. record unresolved uncertainty;
8. let Adel make the final translation decision;
9. update this file when the conceptual analysis materially advances;
10. update `GLOSSARY.md` when a practical recurring Persian equivalent is established.

The mentor must not silently treat a contextual proposal as established historical fact.

---

## 9. Core principle

> Preserve the historical identity of a German concept when that identity matters to the author's argument.

But also:

> Do not preserve German terminology mechanically when accurate and readable Persian can carry the same conceptual function.

The goal is historically informed, conceptually precise, and readable scholarly Persian.
