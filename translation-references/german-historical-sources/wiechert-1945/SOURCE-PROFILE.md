## Bibliographic relationship to the translation project

Stephen Brockmann, “German Culture at the ‘Zero Hour’,” in *Revisiting Zero Hour 1945: The Emergence of Postwar German Culture*, edited by Stephen Brockmann and Frank Trommler, uses and quotes Wiechert's 1945 speech in C01. Brockmann's endnote 4 identifies:

> Ernst Wiechert, *Rede an die Deutsche Jugend 1945* (Munich: Zinnen, 1945), p. 33 — cited in *Auf der Suche nach der Stunde Null: Literatur und Alltag 1945* (Bad Salzdetfurth: Barbara Franzbecker, 1991), p. 20.

This is a **bibliographic chain supplied by Brockmann**, not independent identification of the user's 2023 PDF with that printed page. PDF page 9 of the supplied 2023 file is not “page 33” of the 1945 edition.

Brockmann then quotes Erich Kuby's response. The German Wiechert speech supports contextual study of that controversy, but is **not a substitute for Kuby's own German text** (*Die erste und einzige Rede deutscher Jugend an ihren Dichter*, published under Alexander Parlach).

## Scope, contents and useful page locators

- **PDF pages 1–4:** Opening account of Germany, the First World War, propaganda, National Socialist symbols, responsibility and early persecution.
- **PDF pages 5–8:** Twelve years of wrongdoing, witnesses, prisoners, complicity, the war and moral collapse.
- **PDF page 9:** The lines beginning `Da stehen wir nun vor dem verlassenen Haus`; `So allein`; `So gebrandmarkt`; `Was sollen wir tun?`; the answer including `Laßt uns ... einen neuen Grenzstein vor einem neuen Feld`.
- **PDF page 10:** Continued call to responsibility and repentance; *Johannes-Evangelium*; words versus love; a new beginning.
- **PDF page 11:** Teaching German youth, suffering, testimony from detention, love, responsibility, and the younger generation.
- **PDF page 12:** Moral instruction, forgiveness, renewal; a four-line quotation from verse and a repeated two-line quotation.
- **PDF pages 13–14:** Closing poem beginning `Es geht ein Pflüger übers Land`.

These locators refer only to **this 14-page PDF** and its derived Markdown page markers.

## Extraction / transcription policy

- **Operation:** faithful structural extraction from selectable PDF text into UTF-8 Markdown; no OCR, translation, paraphrase, historical modernization, semantic editing, or inferred corrections.
- **Coverage:** all 14 PDF pages; speech text, closing poem and the title's footnote included.
- **Page tracking:** `<!-- Source PDF page N of 14 -->` comments preserve locators without becoming an invented part of the speech.
- **Paragraphs:** extracted PDF text blocks retained as paragraphs. A single paragraph divided by a physical PDF page boundary remains divided by the page comment; do not mistake that break for an authorial paragraph break.
- **Prose line wraps:** PDF line breaks inside the same text block were joined with spaces. **141 apparent end-of-line discretionary hyphens** were removed when reconnecting a word broken between two lines. The printed PDF remains authoritative if any dehyphenation is disputed.
- **Poetry:** poetic lineation preserved explicitly using Markdown hard breaks; stanzas retained as separate blocks. The page-13-to-14 poem split is a PDF page boundary, not a poetic break.
- **Title and footnote:** `1945` has a footnote marker in the PDF title. Markdown uses `[^1]` with the original footnote wording moved to the end for readability.
- **Running matter:** repetitive running footer `Wiechert - Rede an die deutsche Jugend 1945`, PDF page number and `30.06.2023` are not mixed into the speech body. Their information is preserved in this profile and in page comments.
- **German orthography:** original spellings such as `daß`, `Laßt`, `Mißgunst`, `Geßlerhüten` and `büßen` remain unchanged. Original punctuation/capitalization is retained, including apparently unusual passages.

### Quality control performed

- Programmatic PDF inspection: 14 pages, selectable text, no OCR required.
- Programmatic extraction and comparison of every included source text block to its Markdown transformation.
- 48 prose text blocks and 11 poetry/indented quotation blocks included.
- 14 page markers present; manual checks at the important page-9 quotation, the page-10 Johannine reference, and the end of the poem.

**Verification level:** `machine-extracted / block-compared / selected passage checked`. This is **not** a claim of complete human proof-reading against every PDF line, nor of comparison to the original 1945 printing.

### Source anomalies left unresolved, not silently corrected

The 2023 PDF contains forms requiring independent checking before creating a definitive scholarly edition. Examples include a possible missing stop in `die Herzen hungerten nach Brot Eine große Zeit` (PDF p. 1), spacing in `Johannes- Evangeliums` (PDF p. 10), and `TotenwaldLager` (PDF p. 11). The Markdown preserves the extracted forms. The original 1945 edition should decide whether these are transcription/editorial artifacts.

## Usage rule for the Translation Mentor

1. English Brockmann is the **primary text being translated into Persian**.
2. Use this German Wiechert text to verify quoted German diction, rhetorical images, and historical context, **not** to override the English text silently.
3. If English and German differ, document the difference and make a provisional recommendation; the translator must approve any consequential rendering.
4. Do not attribute the English rendering of Kuby's words to this file. Kuby's own text remains a separate reference gap.
5. For a contested detail, cite both the PDF page marker and the exact German wording; inspect the supplied PDF and, when necessary, the original 1945 printed edition.
6. This profile is source provenance and usage guidance, **not** a translation approval or a German–Persian glossary entry.
